\subsection{Interpretation of Results}
The sentiment and toxicity analyses show that most Wikipedia Talk
page discussions in our dataset were labeled as Neutral, even for
topics that are commonly viewed as political or controversial. This
suggests that editors generally communicate in a measured way and
follow Wikipedia’s expectations for civil discussion. Toxicity levels
were also consistently low, which further supports the idea that the
platform’s community guidelines help shape constructive dialogue.

At the same time, the results vary depending on the number of
comments available for each topic. Pages with many comments,
such as \emph{Capitalism}, provide stronger evidence of stable and
neutral discussion patterns. In contrast, topics with only a few
comments (for example, \emph{Gentrification} or \emph{Atheism})
do not offer enough data to draw firm conclusions. In these cases,
high neutrality percentages may simply reflect limited participation
rather than the presence of consensus.

Overall, our findings suggest that while the tone of Talk page
discussions tends to be neutral, this does not automatically mean
that the underlying decision-making processes are broad or
representative.

\subsection{Implications}
These findings have two main implications for understanding 
Wikipedia as a collaborative platform. First, the neutral tone of
discussions suggests that Wikipedia’s policies, such as assuming
good faith and working toward consensus, appear to influence how
editors interact. This is encouraging for the quality of article
content, as constructive discussion is often linked to more accurate
and balanced writing.

Second, however, neutrality in tone does not guarantee democratic
participation. Several Talk pages were shaped by only a few editors,
which means that decision-making may still be concentrated among
a small group. This aligns with existing research showing that
online communities can develop informal hierarchies over time.
Therefore, it is important to think about both \emph{how} editors
communicate and \emph{who} participates when evaluating Wikipedia’s
governance.

For broader digital platforms, these patterns highlight the challenge
of supporting both civil communication and inclusive participation.
A platform may succeed at encouraging respectful dialogue but still
struggle to involve a diverse group of contributors.

\subsection{Limitations}
This study has several limitations. The number of comments varied
greatly from topic to topic, which affects how much we can infer
from each case. Some pages had only two or three comments, while
others had dozens. The sentiment and toxicity models we used are
also general-purpose tools, which means they might miss subtleties
in Wikipedia conversations, such as sarcasm or complex arguments.

Additionally, our analysis focused only on English-language
Wikipedia and a limited set of topics. Other language communities
may have different norms, participation patterns, or conflict
dynamics. Finally, while we measured the number of contributors,
we did not study their roles or influence in detail. A deeper analysis
of editor networks would help clarify how decisions are made.

\subsection{Future Work}
Future work could expand both the methods and the scope of this
study. More advanced language models could help capture context
and detect subtle forms of bias more reliably. Another useful
direction would be a temporal analysis that follows discussions over
time to see how tone or toxicity shifts during major events or
controversies.

Additional research could also examine how editors interact with
one another by mapping participation networks, which would help
reveal whether certain contributors consistently guide discussions.
Comparing different language Wikipedias would provide insight into
how culture and community norms shape governance. Finally,
connecting Talk page discussions directly to changes in article
content would show more clearly how deliberation influences
Wikipedia’s final output.
